What Monsoon Dust Storms Do to Your Panels

A single haboob can coat an array in fine silt. Here is how to plan your cleanings around storm season…

Marcus Hale
Founder, Dunebright Solar
July 22, 2026
1 min read

Every summer, towering walls of dust roll across the Valley. They are spectacular to watch and rough on solar panels, leaving a fine, even layer of silt that is easy to miss from the ground.

Here is what that dust does, and how to time your cleanings around it.

From Dust to Mud

A haboob drops fine silt on everything. A light shower afterward turns it into a thin mud that bakes hard in the sun. A few rounds of that and the glass is covered in a haze that blocks a real share of your sunlight.

July Through September

Clouds and heat already lower production in late summer, so dust is easy to overlook. Many owners blame the weather when dirty glass is a big part of the gap.

A quick check

  • Compare this month with the same month last year
  • Look for a drop bigger than the weather explains
  • Look at the panels at a low angle early in the morning

When to Book

Book a cleaning in late September or October, once storm season winds down. Homes near open desert or farmland often benefit from an extra visit in midsummer.

Put the hose down. Hosing off storm dust with tap water just swaps mud for mineral spots. Purified water rinses it all away and dries clear.

Three Signs Your Panels Are Overdue for a Cleaning

Panels can look fine from the driveway and still be losing power. Here is how to spot the warning signs before they show up on your bill.

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

From the street, almost every solar array looks clean. That is exactly why so many owners go years without a cleaning, and quietly lose money the whole time.

Dust builds up in a thin, even layer. You will not notice it from 20 feet away, but it scatters sunlight before it reaches the cells, and the system slowly produces less while nobody is watching.

We clean a lot of roofs around the Valley, and these are the three signs we see most often on systems that are overdue.

1. Your App Shows Lower Numbers

Your monitoring app is the best early warning you have. If this month is running 5 to 10 percent behind the same month last year, and the weather has been similar, dust is the most likely reason.

How to check: Open your inverter app and compare the same month across two years. Panels only lose around half a percent a year to age, so a drop of 5 percent or more on similar sunny days usually points to dirty glass.

On systems that looked fine to their owners, we regularly see output jump 12 to 25 percent after one cleaning. Every day before that is power you paid for and did not get.

2. You Can See Dust, Spots or Droppings

If dirt is visible from the ground, the buildup up close is worse than it looks. Anything you can spot from below has usually been collecting for months.

What we find on Valley roofs

  • Bird droppings leave shaded spots that can overheat and damage cells over time
  • Storm dust from summer haboobs settles as an even haze that is hard to see but easy to measure
  • Spring pollen from palo verde and mesquite leaves a sticky yellow film
  • Job-site dust from new subdivisions bakes onto hot glass and gets harder to remove

A quick warning: Skip the garden hose. Valley tap water leaves mineral spots, and spraying cold water on a hot panel can stress the glass. Professional crews use purified water and clean during cooler hours.

3. It Has Been Over Six Months

Even if nothing looks wrong, six months is a long time for a desert roof. Dust, pollen and at least one storm season have almost certainly passed through.

Many panel makers also mention routine cleaning with approved methods in their maintenance guidelines. Keeping a record of regular service is a cheap way to protect a system that cost tens of thousands of dollars.

What we suggest: For most homes, once after spring pollen and once after monsoon season. Commercial roofs and homes next to open desert or construction do better with quarterly visits.

The Short Version

Dirty panels are an easy problem to fix. For most homes, the power recovered by one cleaning covers its cost within a few months.

"A Chandler business we work with had not cleaned its 200-panel roof in three years. Daily production went up 22 percent after our first visit."

If you are not sure, check your app or ask us to take a look. It is easier to stay ahead of dust than to catch up.

Why We Only Clean With Purified Water

Hard tap water leaves white spots that block light. Here is how purified water fixes that…

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

Valley tap water is full of dissolved minerals. When it dries on hot glass, those minerals stay behind as white spots that block light. Purified water has those minerals removed before it ever reaches your panels.

It is a simple idea, and you can see the difference as soon as the glass dries.

What Purified Water Means

Our filters pull calcium, magnesium and other dissolved solids out of the water. What comes out the other side measures close to zero on a dissolved-solids meter.

We test it with a handheld meter at the start of every job, so we know exactly what is touching your glass.

The Problem With Tap Water

Phoenix water is among the hardest in the country. Rinse a hot panel with a hose and the water evaporates in seconds, leaving a ring of minerals behind. Do it a few times and the spots start to stack up.

Why spots matter

  • They block and scatter sunlight
  • They give dust something to stick to
  • They get harder to remove the longer they sit

How Pure Water Cleans

Water with nothing in it naturally pulls dirt into itself. Paired with a soft brush, it lifts dust off the glass and rinses away clean, and because it carries no minerals, it dries clear without wiping.

"Look at the panels on a sunny afternoon and you can tell. No spots, no streaks, no film."

Bottom Line

When you get quotes, ask each company what water they use and whether they test it. A good crew will have an easy answer.

Can the Wrong Cleaning Hurt Your Warranty?

Pressure washers and hard water can cause damage a manufacturer won't cover. Here is what to know…

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

A pressure washer or a stiff brush might seem like a quick fix, but damage from the wrong cleaning method is often excluded from panel warranties.

So yes, it can matter. How much depends on your warranty terms and what caused the damage.

What the Fine Print Covers

Every brand words it differently, but most exclude damage from improper maintenance, abrasive tools or harsh chemicals.

What to Avoid

  • Pressure washers: They can push water past seals and into the frame
  • Hard water: Mineral spots build up with every rinse
  • Abrasive brushes or pads: Scratches never come back out
  • Cold water on hot glass: A sudden temperature swing stresses the glass
  • Harsh detergents: Strong cleaners can wear down the coating

How to Stay Covered

Check the manual

Look for the maintenance section and note any approved methods, water guidance or suggested intervals.

Keep a service log

Save the date, the method and the company for each cleaning. Our photo reports make this easy if you ever need to file a claim.

Ask Before You Guess

If the language is unclear, a quick call to your installer or the manufacturer now beats arguing over a denied claim later.

A Facilities Manager's Checklist for Solar Cleaning Contracts

A vague cleaning clause can leave you paying for less than you think. Here is what to put in writing…

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

Plenty of solar maintenance contracts spell out inverter checks in detail and then mention cleaning in a single line. That leaves a lot of room for missed visits and shortcuts.

Use this list the next time your contract comes up for renewal.

How Often and How

"As needed" means your vendor decides. Put a schedule and a method in writing instead.

  • A fixed number of visits per year that fits your site
  • Purified water and soft brushes named as the method
  • Production data before and after each visit

Safety Paperwork

If someone gets hurt on your roof, your organization is involved. Get the paperwork before work starts.

What to ask for

  • Current liability and workers' comp certificates
  • OSHA training records for the assigned crew
  • A fall protection plan written for your roof
  • A named supervisor for every visit

Proof of Work

You should never wonder whether a visit happened. Ask for dated photos, before-and-after production numbers and notes on any damage the crew finds.

Warranty Alignment

Match the cleaning method in your contract to what your panel manufacturer recommends, and keep every service record together.

"Find the gaps in your contract before you need it, not after."

Why Desert Panels Get Dirtier Than Most

National soiling research puts the desert Southwest near the top of the list. Here is why…

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

Arizona gets more sun than almost anywhere in the country, which is great for solar. The same dry climate is tough on panel glass, because dust and pollen can pile up for months between real rains.

National soiling maps back up what we see on roofs every week: losses here run higher than most of the country and climb quickly during dry months.

Why the Desert Is Different

Three things work against panels in the Valley.

  • Long dry spells: Months can go by without a soaking rain
  • Haboobs: Summer storms carry fine silt for miles
  • Light showers: A sprinkle turns loose dust into stuck-on film

Rain Is Not a Cleaning

People often ask whether a good storm will do the job for free. Usually it does the opposite: the water carries dirt to the bottom edge of each panel and leaves it there.

Watch the bottom edge

A strip of dirt along the frame shades the lowest row of cells, and depending on the wiring, that can drag down a whole string of panels.

What Helps

Plan cleanings around the seasons instead of waiting for visible dirt. For most homes that means one visit after spring pollen and one after monsoon season.

Commercial roofs and homes near open land or construction usually do best with quarterly service. Your monitoring app will tell you whether the schedule is working.

What Happens to Output After a Cleaning

We compared inverter data from a dozen commercial jobs before and after cleaning. Here are the numbers…

Marcus Hale
July 22, 2026
1 min read

Everyone says clean panels make more power. We wanted real numbers, so we looked at inverter data from 12 commercial jobs and compared two weeks before each cleaning with two weeks after.

On average, output rose 17 percent. A few sites gained much more and a few gained less, mostly depending on how long they had gone without a cleaning.

How We Ran the Numbers

Weather can skew any comparison, so we adjusted every result for peak sun hours and left out cloudy days and days with equipment faults.

  • Sample: 12 commercial roofs and shade canopies around the Valley
  • Window: Two weeks before and two weeks after
  • Metric: Energy produced per peak sun hour

What We Found

Sites that had gone a year or more without cleaning gained about 23 percent. Sites on a six-month schedule gained about 9 percent. Arrays near freeways, farms or construction saw the biggest jumps.

Small gains still add up

Nine percent sounds small, but on a 500 kW roof it is a lot of energy, and it keeps paying off until the panels get dirty again.

Try It Yourself

You can run the same test at home. Note your production for two weeks before your next cleaning, then compare it with the two weeks after.

"Customers on a regular schedule never see a big jump, because they never fall that far behind."
