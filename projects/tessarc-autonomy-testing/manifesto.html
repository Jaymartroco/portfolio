Manifesto

The test before the launch

The hard part of shipping autonomous machines is no longer building them. It’s proving they’re ready.

Proving a machine means gathering evidence of how it works, how it compares with alternatives and how it should be used. Every operator has to answer these questions before a robot or vehicle goes to work. Software-defined machines now change so often that our ability to validate them has fallen behind, and that gap is quietly becoming a safety problem.

Validation hasn’t sped up because it was never built to. For decades, testing assumed a machine was a fixed product with a stable configuration, working inside a known envelope against conditions that stayed the same for years. Under those rules it made sense to test occasionally and judge each product on its own. Today a delivery robot, an inspection drone or a driver-assist stack still moves through a string of disconnected trials, often spread over years, before it reaches customers.

That rhythm fit the era of fixed hardware, but autonomous machines break it. Their behaviour lives in software that changes constantly, and each release can change how a vehicle steers, what a sensor notices or how an operator decides. That speed should be an advantage, but without a way to validate it just as fast, teams treat it as a risk. They are stuck choosing between holding back improvements and shipping on untested assumptions.

Nobody feels this more than the engineers running these trials. Without modern tooling, test teams patch together their own setups from custom scripts, scoring sheets, scenarios and hazard models. Distances and timings are often estimated by watching a screen, logged in spreadsheets and chat threads, then summarised over months in slide decks. In many cases the raw data behind major, expensive decisions is never captured at all.

None of this is the fault of the builders or the testers; both do impressive work with what they have. But decision makers end up sorting through stacks of reports that don’t give them the clarity they need. Machines get approved on claimed performance instead of measured data, and when they fall short, the cost is lost trust, lost time and real safety risk.

This is the test before the launch: learning what works before customers find out the hard way. It won’t be solved by running the old process faster. It will be solved by validating machines at the pace they are built, with evidence captured the same way at every site, so everyone shares an honest, current picture of what works.

We started Tessarc to build the platform that produces that evidence, together with the teams doing this work every day.

Generating hard evidence

Hard evidence is the record of what really happened. We want it so decisions rest on facts rather than claims.

Capturing it is harder than it looks. The instruments must be more precise than the machine being tested, and every scenario measures something different, so each needs its own careful setup. Real-world results also depend on many machines sensing, talking and acting together, which means data from each one has to be collected and synced before anyone can say how a single machine, or a single setting, changed the outcome.

In effect, this is integration testing for the physical world. Software teams solved it long ago with stable virtual environments where many services run together and spin up and down with every code change. The physical version follows the same idea, combining many machines to see whether the whole works, but without the stability of a virtual setup. We can’t control gusts of wind or a failing part mid-test, but we can build a repeatable harness for instrumentation, data capture and analysis. Tessarc builds the open foundation for that harness, which we call the Proving Lab.

A Proving Lab has three layers.

Layer 1

Environment

The first layer is the Environment, built to give machines under test an experience that matches real operating conditions. An incomplete environment produces incomplete evidence. The most realistic setting is the real world itself, which is why so many teams travel to test on site. The Proving Lab brings that realism to one place as closely as possible, then goes further by simulating new variations.

Layer 2

Measurement

The second layer is Measurement. Every meaningful action by a machine or an operator is captured precisely and synced to one clock, so a detailed timeline can be rebuilt. In place of eyeballed estimates and cherry-picked metrics, the Proving Lab instruments the whole environment and merges every feed into one picture, scored against shared standards.

Layer 3

Learning

The third layer is Learning, where measurements are filtered and combined to answer real questions. AI assistants surface the patterns that matter, such as how new releases affect results or which behaviours lead to success. They can also replay what-ifs, like what would have changed if a sensor sat on the other side of the site or an obstacle behaved differently.

Together, the three layers turn a single trial into evidence that builds over time. The Proving Lab is portable by design, so its hardware and software can be set up wherever teams test or operate. It is also open, so the best instruments for each scenario can plug in, the same way operators deserve the best tools for the job.

Earning trust

Hard evidence can be uncomfortable. It can expose weak spots in a product or approach that its champions defend. That is why evidence has to stay separate from advocacy, and why we watch for incentives that get in the way of honest answers.

As founders, we have seen this up close while building robots, safety-critical hardware and data platforms at fast-growing technology companies. We have sat on the builder’s side, watching big decisions made without clear evidence, and we have met many people working hard to do it properly even when shortcuts are rewarded.

So we set up Tessarc as an independent company whose core business never overlaps with the machines tested in the Proving Lab. Our partners always decide what gets approved and what ships. Tessarc provides the evidence through methods anyone can inspect, and lets the data speak for itself. No model is perfect, but we believe this one is stronger than vendors marking their own homework, pairing independence with the scale of a real product.

Ship what works

To the teams building a better way to validate machines, who know that products now improve continuously against shifting requirements and that most processes haven’t caught up yet: Tessarc was built for you. Together we can make validation that keeps pace with the product and stands behind every answer.

The test before the launch is already underway. The teams that win will be the ones that tested honestly first. We started Tessarc to help them get there.

See you in the Proving Lab.

~ The Tessarc team
